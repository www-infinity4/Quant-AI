What can I do with a Quant?

1 · Collect

Keep Quants that match something useful: an artist, product, hobby, place, material or buying interest.

2 · Organize

Put like-minded Quants into folders and clusters. Pink Floyd + Steve Miller + similar signals can become a Rock Music collection.

3 · Discover

Use a Quant's one permitted search to ask where compatible anonymous demand exists. Results expose allowed signals, not personal histories.

4 · Build

Turn a cluster into a practical plan: what to stock, how many units to test, likely audience fit and what evidence supports the idea.

5 · Reach

Offer something to compatible rotating wallet aliases. The customer decides whether to engage; the merchant does not receive the person's identity.

Example

Six albums → a tiny inventory business

You collect music-related Quants and organize them into a rock folder. Quant-AI finds permitted anonymous signals showing interest around that category. Instead of buying 100 copies blindly, you might test six albums at dealer pricing, record cost and target margin, and offer them to compatible anonymous audiences.

The Quant is evidence for a decision—not a guarantee of a sale. Quant-AI should show why it suggested the inventory and let the owner decide.

Privacy contract

No permanent merchant tracking ID

  • Your unified-wallet identity remains private and stable only inside the trusted wallet system.
  • Checkout and merchant discovery use scoped, rotating aliases rather than the private wallet ID.
  • A merchant cannot use checkout to attach your name to a permanent public wallet identifier.
  • Quant provenance remains verifiable even when the merchant-facing alias rotates.
  • Search returns only the minimum allowed signal needed for the requested purpose—never a person's history.
